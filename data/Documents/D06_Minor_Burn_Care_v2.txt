Document Title: BasicCare Minor Burn Care Guidelines
Document ID: D06
Version: 2.0
Effective Date: June 2025

Purpose
This document describes appropriate first-aid steps for minor burns, such as small superficial burns from cooking or brief contact with a hot surface.

Definition of Minor Burn
A minor burn typically affects only the outer layer of skin, causing redness, mild swelling, and localized pain, without blistering over a large area.

Immediate First-Aid Steps
Move the affected area away from the heat source right away. Gently remove any nearby clothing or jewelry before swelling develops, taking care not to disturb the burned skin.

Cooling Procedure
Hold the burn under cool running water for approximately 15 minutes. This is generally sufficient to relieve pain and reduce further tissue damage. Prolonged cooling beyond this point offers little additional benefit and may increase discomfort from prolonged cold exposure.

What Not to Do
Avoid applying home remedies such as butter, oils, or ice directly to the burn, as these can trap heat or damage the skin further. Do not intentionally pop blisters, since intact skin acts as a natural barrier against infection.

When to Seek Professional Care
Seek professional evaluation for burns larger than the size of a palm, burns involving the face, hands, feet, or joints, burns that appear deep or discolored, or any signs of infection such as worsening redness, swelling, or discharge.

Special Considerations
Children and older adults should be monitored closely after any burn, as healing can take longer and complications may develop more quickly in these groups.
