Document Title: BasicCare Fever & Temperature Guidelines
Document ID: D02
Version: 2.0
Effective Date: January 2025

Purpose
This document provides general guidance for BasicCare members on recognizing and managing fever at home. It is intended for educational use within the BasicCare Health & Wellness Handbook and does not replace individualized medical advice.

Definition of Fever
A fever is generally defined as a body temperature above the normal range, most often measured orally or under the arm. Mild elevations are common and often resolve without intervention.

General Self-Care
Members experiencing a mild fever are encouraged to rest, stay in a cool and well-ventilated room, and wear breathable clothing. Adequate hydration is important throughout the illness, and small frequent meals are recommended over large ones. Over-the-counter fever reducers may be used according to package instructions if the member has no contraindicating conditions.

Temperature Monitoring
Temperature should be checked every four hours during an active fever episode using a reliable thermometer, with readings recorded to track the overall trend.

When to Seek Professional Help
Professional evaluation should be considered when a member's temperature reaches 38.5°C, or if fever persists beyond two days, is accompanied by severe headache, rash, difficulty breathing, or unusual drowsiness.

Special Considerations
Infants, older adults, and individuals with weakened immune systems should be evaluated more promptly than the general population, since fever can indicate a more serious underlying issue in these groups. Caregivers are encouraged to seek guidance early rather than waiting for symptoms to worsen.
