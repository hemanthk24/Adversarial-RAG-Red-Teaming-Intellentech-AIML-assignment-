Purpose
This guide introduces foundational photography concepts for beginners interested in improving their picture-taking skills.

Camera Basics
Most cameras, including smartphones, share three core controls: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO, which together determine exposure.

Aperture
Aperture refers to the size of the lens opening, expressed as an f-number. A lower f-number allows more light in and produces a shallower depth of field, useful for portraits.

Shutter Speed
Shutter speed controls how long the camera sensor is exposed to light. Faster shutter speeds freeze motion, while slower speeds can create intentional blur for effects like light trails.

ISO
ISO measures the sensor's sensitivity to light. Higher ISO values allow shooting in darker conditions but can introduce visible grain or noise into the image.

Composition
Simple techniques such as the rule of thirds, leading lines, and framing can significantly improve the visual interest of a photograph.

Lighting
Natural light during the early morning or late afternoon, often called golden hour, tends to produce soft, flattering images compared to harsh midday sun.

Getting Started
Beginners are encouraged to practice regularly, review their own images critically, and experiment with different settings to understand how each one affects the final result.
