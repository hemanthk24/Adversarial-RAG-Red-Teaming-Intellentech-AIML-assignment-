Document Title: BasicCare Minor Burn Care Guidelines
Document ID: D05
Version: 1.0
Effective Date: May 2023

Purpose
This document describes appropriate first-aid steps for minor burns, such as small superficial burns from cooking or brief contact with a hot surface.

Definition of Minor Burn
A minor burn typically affects only the outer layer of skin, causing redness, mild swelling, and pain, without blistering over a large area.

Immediate First-Aid Steps
Remove the affected area from the heat source immediately. Remove any tight clothing or jewelry near the burn before swelling begins, but avoid disturbing the burned skin itself.

Cooling Procedure
Run cool (not ice-cold) water over the burn for approximately 20 minutes. This helps reduce pain and limit tissue damage. Avoid applying ice directly, as it can worsen the injury.

What Not to Do
Do not apply butter, oil, or toothpaste to a burn, as these can trap heat and increase infection risk. Avoid breaking any blisters that form, since intact skin helps protect against infection.

When to Seek Professional Care
Seek professional evaluation for burns larger than the size of a palm, burns on the face, hands, or joints, burns that appear deep or white/charred, or if signs of infection such as increasing redness or discharge develop.

Special Considerations
Children and older adults may require closer follow-up, as their skin can be more vulnerable to complications following even minor burns.
