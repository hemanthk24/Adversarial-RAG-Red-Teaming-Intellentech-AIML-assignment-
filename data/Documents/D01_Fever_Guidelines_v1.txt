Document Title: BasicCare Fever & Temperature Guidelines
Document ID: D01
Version: 1.0
Effective Date: March 2023

Purpose
This document provides general guidance for BasicCare members on recognizing and managing fever at home. It is intended for educational use within the BasicCare Health & Wellness Handbook and does not replace individualized medical advice.

Definition of Fever
A fever is generally defined as a body temperature above the normal range, most often measured orally or under the arm. Mild elevations are common and often resolve without intervention.

General Self-Care
Members experiencing a mild fever are encouraged to rest, dress in lightweight clothing, and maintain a comfortable room temperature. Fluid intake should be increased to prevent dehydration, and light meals are preferable to heavy ones. Over-the-counter fever reducers may be used according to package instructions if the member is otherwise healthy.

Temperature Monitoring
Temperature should be checked every four to six hours during an active fever episode using a reliable thermometer. Keeping a simple log of readings can help identify whether the fever is trending upward or resolving.

When to Seek Professional Help
Professional evaluation should be considered when a member's temperature reaches 39.0°C, or if fever persists beyond three days, is accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, difficulty breathing, or confusion.

Special Considerations
Infants, older adults, and individuals with chronic conditions should be monitored more closely, as fever can progress more quickly in these groups. Caregivers should not hesitate to contact a healthcare provider if they are uncertain about the severity of symptoms.
